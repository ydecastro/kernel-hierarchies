# kernel-hierarchies

Code and figures for the paper

> Yohann De Castro, **Kernel Hierarchies for Global Optimisation**, preprint, 2026.

The paper lifts the moment–sum-of-squares (Lasserre) hierarchy from the monomial basis to a nested family of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces generated by partial Taylor truncations of a universal kernel (the Gaussian as the guiding example), with kernel mean embeddings in the role of moments. This repository contains the Jupyter notebooks that produce every figure and every number quoted in the paper, their stored outputs, and the figures.

Repository: <https://github.com/ydecastro/kernel-hierarchies>

## Layout

```
code/                    notebooks (outputs stored), requirements.txt
code/bench_rate_results/ cached certified brackets used by bench_rate.ipynb
figs/                    the paper's figures (PDF), README.txt
figs/extra/              further figures produced by the benchmark and robustness notebooks
```

| Notebook | Paper | What it does | Run time (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Kernel-Hierarchies.ipynb` | whole paper | Companion tutorial on small examples (kernels, moment cone, the constant function, the relaxation, flat extension, Christoffel strengthening, a 1-D sketched BLASSO), and a table checking the numbers quoted in the paper against the stored outputs of the other notebooks | 5 s (`RUN_FULL = False`) |
| `bench_rate.ipynb` | §5.5, Figure 1 | Convergence rate of Theorem 14: certified brackets for λ_{d,n} and ρ_{d,n} with its own primal–dual interior-point solver in a tensor Chebyshev basis; draws `figs/fig_rate.pdf` | 4 s from the cache (`RECOMPUTE = False`) |
| `bench_rate_check.ipynb` | §5.5, Figure 1 | Independent cross-check of `bench_rate.ipynb`: collocation formulation in a Legendre basis, own interior-point solver in float64 and 40-digit arithmetic, exact rational verification on the interval | about 50 min |
| `bench_global_opt.ipynb` | §5.7, Figure 2 | Numerical illustration of the global-minimisation hierarchy (§4–§5): bounds, flat truncation and extraction, the ball-constraint remark, the non-compact case; draws `figs/fig_global_opt.pdf` | about 1 min |
| `bench_comparison.ipynb` | §5.7, Table 1 | Comparison with the standard Lasserre hierarchy at equal moment-matrix size, and conditioning of the moment matrices | about 6 min |
| `fig_blasso_2dgmm.ipynb` | §6.5, Figures 3–4 | Two-dimensional Gaussian mixture: recovery at two bands, singular values, dual certificates | about 10 s |
| `fig_deconv_2d.ipynb` | §6.6, Figures 5–7 | Off-the-grid deconvolution of a signed measure, SNR sweep, rank-tolerance sensitivity, Christoffel (H1) correction | several minutes |
| `robustness_sec6.ipynb` | §6.5–§6.6 | Robustness of the §6 experiments over 20 draws per point (the numbers quoted in the paper) | 15–25 min |

Run times were measured on a 10-core laptop (Apple silicon).

## Running the notebooks

Tested with Python 3.13.3 and the versions listed in `code/requirements.txt`.

```bash
python -m pip install -r code/requirements.txt
cd code
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace fig_blasso_2dgmm.ipynb
```

Execute the notebooks from `code/`: they write the paper's figures to `../figs/` and the other figures to `../figs/extra/`. All random draws use fixed seeds. Solver statuses (for instance `optimal_inaccurate`, or Clarabel failures followed by an SCS fallback) can depend on the versions of cvxpy, Clarabel and SCS; the notebooks print them.

Flags at the top of some notebooks:

- `Kernel-Hierarchies.ipynb`: `RUN_FULL = True` re-executes the other notebooks on copies in a temporary directory; `WRITE_FIGS = True` also copies their figures into `figs/`.
- `bench_rate.ipynb`: `RECOMPUTE = True` recomputes the cached brackets in `code/bench_rate_results/` instead of loading them.
- `robustness_sec6.ipynb`: `FULL = True` gives the 50-draw study stored in `robustness_sec6_full.ipynb`.

## Solvers

Most semidefinite programs are posed with CVXPY and solved with Clarabel, with SCS as a fallback where stated. The high-order relaxations of `bench_rate.ipynb` and `bench_rate_check.ipynb` use their own interior-point solvers in orthogonal (Chebyshev, Legendre) bases, because monomial-basis solves lose accuracy at large relaxation orders.
